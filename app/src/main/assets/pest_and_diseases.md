# Detailed Knowledge Base

## Golden Apple Snail

- **Category:** Pest
- **Main Stage:** Seedling
- **Typical Risk Window:** Young rice and seedlings, especially under 30 days old

### Symptoms

- Missing rice hills
- Cut rice stems at the base
- Damaged or cut leaves
- Bright pink egg masses
- Whole seedlings destroyed, especially under 30 days old

### Notes

Golden apple snails eat young and emerging rice plants. They spread through irrigation canals, natural water pathways, and flooding. Damage is most severe in direct wet-seeded rice and transplanted rice up to 30 days old. Rice plants become more resistant around 30–40 days old.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Handpick snails and crush eggs
- Place bamboo stakes for egg collection
- Use attractant plants such as papaya or cassava leaves
- Keep water level below 2 cm during early crop stages
- Build canalettes to drain water and trap snails
- Block field entry with screens or mesh at water inlets and outlets
- Transplant 25–30 day-old seedlings from low-density nurseries
- Plant multiple seedlings per hill

#### Biological Control

- Encourage ducks, red ants, rats, and birds to feed on snails or eggs
- Let ducks forage in the field after 30–35 days
- Snails may be harvested for food or feed with care due to health risk

#### Use of Toxic Plants

- Place tobacco leaves, citrus leaves, or heartleaf false pickerelweed across fields

#### Chemical Control

- Use molluscicides only during seedling phase under 30 days old
- Apply only in canalettes or low spots
- Follow safe application practices
- Choose products with low human and environmental toxicity

## Bacterial Leaf Blight

- **Category:** Bacterial
- **Stages Mentioned:** Seedling, Tillering, Stem Elongation, Panicle Initiation, Booting, Milking, Dough, Mature

### Symptoms

- Seedlings: grey-green streaks from tips or margins that turn yellow-white with wavy edges, then wilt and die
- Early infection in transplanted rice: whole plant wilts and dies (kresek)
- Older plants: pale yellow streaks with wavy edges that dry and die
- Later infection at booting stage: poor grain quality
- Milky droplets may ooze from streaks and dry as crust
- Streaks may appear on leaf sheaths and stems

### Notes

Bacterial leaf blight is caused by _Xanthomonas oryzae_ pv. _oryzae_. It survives on grass weeds and infected stubbles. It spreads through wind, rain splash, and irrigation water. Disease is favored by frequent rainfall, strong winds, humidity above 70%, warm temperatures of 25°C to 34°C, heavy nitrogen fertilization, and close planting. Early infection causes higher yield loss.

### Prevention and Control

#### Biosecurity

- Use certified BLB-free seed
- Quarantine and test imported rice seeds

#### Cultural Control Before Planting

- Select seed only from healthy, BLB-free plants
- Plant resistant rice varieties

#### Cultural Control During Growth

- Avoid injuring seedlings during transplanting
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilizer, around 80–100 kg/ha only
- Maintain good field drainage
- Keep fields weed-free, especially _Leersia_ which can host the pathogen

#### After Harvest

- Plough rice stubble, straw, and ratoons under the soil
- Allow fields to dry or fallow to reduce bacteria

## Caseworm

- **Category:** Pest
- **Main Stage:** Seedling

### Symptoms

- Young larvae scrape leaf surfaces, leaving papery remains
- Rice leaf tips cut at right angles, forming floating leaf cases
- Leaves show ladder-like structures of hard fibers after feeding
- Leaf cases may attach to leaf sheaths or float on water

### Notes

Caseworm is common in standing water in wetland or irrigated fields. It survives on weeds and weedy rice nearby. It is more problematic when young seedlings are transplanted. Poor land preparation and zinc-deficient soils increase risk.

### Prevention and Control

#### Preventive Measures

- Plant early
- Use wider spacing, about 30 × 20 cm
- Transplant older seedlings and destroy remaining eggs
- Drain fields and use filters when re-irrigating after 2–3 days
- Apply fertilizers properly
- Avoid excess nitrogen and ensure adequate potassium
- Remove weeds and weedy rice in and around fields

#### Organic Control

- Encourage snails that eat eggs
- Encourage water beetles that feed on larvae
- Encourage spiders, dragonflies, and birds that feed on adults
- Apply ash or spray neem leaf extract where insects are found

#### Chemical Control

- Use integrated management
- Apply authorized carbamate insecticides if needed
- Avoid pyrethroids because caseworm has developed tolerance

## Rice Blast

- **Category:** Fungus
- **Stages Mentioned:** Seedling, Tillering, Stem Elongation, Panicle Initiation, Booting, Flowering, Milking, Mature

### Symptoms

- Affects leaves, collar, node, neck, panicle, leaf sheath
- Leaves develop yellow to light green eye-shaped lesions with necrotic borders and grey-white centers
- Lesions enlarge and may cause leaf drying and collar rot
- Infected nodes turn brown, causing stem breakage and plant death
- Later infection reduces leaf area, grain fill, and yield

### Notes

Rice blast is caused by _Magnaporthe oryzae_ and can infect rice from seedling to ripening. It is favored by excessive nitrogen fertilizer, cool moist rainy weather, drought stress, and poor drainage.

### Prevention and Control

#### Organic Control

- No fully effective biological control is available yet
- Research is ongoing on _Streptomyces_ and _Pseudomonas_ for suppression

#### Chemical Control

- Use integrated preventive, biological, and chemical management
- Seed treatment with thiram is effective
- Apply fungicides such as azoxystrobin, triazoles, and strobilurins during nursery, tillering, and panicle emergence
- One or two fungicide sprays at heading stage can reduce infection

## Rodents

- **Category:** Pest
- **Stages Mentioned:** Seedling, Tillering, Stem Elongation, Panicle Initiation, Booting, Flowering, Mature

### Symptoms

- Missing or chewed seeds
- Chopped seedlings and missing hills
- Clean cuts through tillers
- Damaged panicles at booting
- Cut or eaten grains at ripening
- Delayed maturity

### Notes

Rice field rats can damage rice at almost all growth stages. Populations grow rapidly where food, water, shelter, double cropping, triple cropping, weedy bunds, and unmanaged field edges are present.

### Prevention and Control

- Synchronize planting within the community
- Clean bunds and field edges
- Maintain physical barriers such as plastic fences or trap barriers
- Conduct community rat control campaigns
- Use traps and fumigation when necessary

## Stem Borer

- **Category:** Pest
- **Stages Mentioned:** Tillering, Stem Elongation, Panicle Initiation, Booting, Flowering, Milking

### Symptoms

- Deadheart during vegetative stage
- Whitehead during reproductive stage
- Egg masses on rice leaves
- Cut stems with larval tunnels inside

### Notes

Stem borer larvae bore into stems and feed inside, cutting the flow of water and nutrients. They survive in stubbles, straw piles, ratoons, and volunteer rice. Continuous planting and poor residue management favor spread.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Plow or rotavate immediately after harvest to destroy stubbles and residues
- Practice synchronous planting within the community
- Use resistant or tolerant varieties when available
- Apply fertilizer properly to maintain vigor
- Monitor fields regularly for egg masses and damage

#### Biological Control

- Conserve egg parasitoids such as _Trichogramma_
- Conserve predators such as spiders, mirid bugs, and ants

#### Chemical Control

- Apply insecticides only when economic threshold is reached, about 1–2 egg masses per m²
- Avoid spraying when whiteheads are already visible because damage has already occurred

## Brown Spot

- **Category:** Fungus
- **Stages Mentioned:** Seedling, Tillering, Panicle Initiation, Booting, Milking

### Symptoms

- Small circular to oval brown lesions on leaves
- Lesions may enlarge with yellow halo or reddish-brown margin
- Seedling blight in nurseries
- Reduced tillering
- Poor grain filling and sometimes empty grains

### Notes

Brown spot is caused by _Bipolaris oryzae_. It attacks rice from seedling to maturity, especially under nutrient deficiency and drought stress. It survives in infected seeds, residues, and soil. Continuous planting and poor soil fertility favor disease.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Use clean and certified seeds
- Apply balanced fertilization, especially enough potassium and silicon
- Avoid excessive nitrogen
- Improve drainage
- Avoid prolonged drought stress
- Practice crop rotation
- Remove infected residues after harvest

#### Biological Control

- Encourage beneficial soil microbes that improve plant health and suppress fungi

#### Chemical Control

- Apply fungicides only when infection is severe and yield loss is expected

## Leaf Folder

- **Category:** Pest
- **Stages Mentioned:** Tillering, Panicle Initiation, Booting, Flowering

### Symptoms

- Leaves folded lengthwise and fastened by silk threads
- White streaks or transparent feeding areas on folded leaves
- Severe infestation causes drying of leaves and reduced photosynthesis
- Greenish caterpillars inside folded leaves

### Notes

Leaf folder larvae hide and feed inside folded leaves. Infestation increases with continuous rice planting, dense canopy, and high nitrogen application.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilizer
- Practice synchronous planting
- Monitor fields regularly for folded leaves and larvae
- Use resistant or tolerant varieties where available

#### Biological Control

- Conserve parasitoid wasps such as _Trichogramma_
- Conserve predators such as spiders and mirid bugs

#### Chemical Control

- Use insecticides only when economic threshold is reached, about 10% of leaves folded at maximum tillering stage
- Apply carefully to avoid harming natural enemies

## Brown Planthopper

- **Category:** Pest
- **Stages Mentioned:** Stem Elongation, Panicle Initiation, Booting, Flowering, Mature

### Symptoms

- Yellowing and wilting starting from the base
- Hopperburn or patches of dead plants
- Stunted growth and poor tillering
- Brown small winged insects at the base of plants

### Notes

Brown planthoppers suck sap from the base of rice stems and can also transmit grassy stunt and ragged stunt viruses. Populations build up rapidly in dense, high-nitrogen fields under continuous cropping.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Avoid excessive nitrogen application
- Plant resistant or tolerant rice varieties
- Practice synchronous planting
- Maintain a fallow period between crops
- Drain fields for a few days when populations are high

#### Biological Control

- Conserve mirid bugs, spiders, and parasitoids that attack eggs and nymphs

#### Chemical Control

- Apply selective insecticides only when populations exceed the economic threshold
- Around 10 insects per hill at early stage
- Around 25–30 insects per hill at later stage
- Avoid unnecessary spraying

## Green Leafhopper

- **Category:** Pest
- **Stages Mentioned:** Tillering, Panicle Initiation

### Symptoms

- Yellowing and stunting
- Hopperburn-like patches under heavy infestation
- Small green wedge-shaped insects on leaves and stems
- Can transmit tungro virus causing yellow to orange leaves and stunting

### Notes

Green leafhoppers feed by sucking plant sap and are major vectors of rice tungro virus. Populations increase with continuous planting, excessive nitrogen, and abundant weeds.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Use resistant or tungro-tolerant varieties
- Practice synchronous planting
- Control weeds in and around fields
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilizer

#### Biological Control

- Encourage spiders, mirid bugs, and egg parasitoids

#### Chemical Control

- Use insecticides only when populations are high and tungro transmission risk is present
- Apply recommended products carefully to protect natural enemies

## False Smut

- **Category:** Fungus
- **Stages Mentioned:** Booting, Flowering, Milking, Dough

### Symptoms

- Yellowish-green spore balls on spikelets
- Swollen grains replaced by velvety green fungal masses
- Infected grains fail to develop
- Panicles may appear discolored

### Notes

False smut is caused by _Ustilaginoidea virens_. It affects rice from booting to maturity and is favored by high humidity and excess nitrogen. The pathogen survives in soil and infected grains and spreads through air, rain, and irrigation water.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Plant resistant varieties
- Use certified seeds
- Avoid excessive nitrogen and maintain balanced fertilization
- Practice crop rotation and field sanitation

#### Biological Control

- Apply biocontrol agents such as _Trichoderma_ in soil

#### Chemical Control

- Spray fungicides during booting and heading stages

## Sheath Rot

- **Category:** Fungus
- **Main Stage:** Flowering
- **Infection Window Mentioned:** Booting to heading

### Symptoms

- Irregular brown lesions on the leaf sheath enclosing the panicle
- Rotting panicles with unfilled or discolored grains
- Panicles fail to emerge properly, also called choked panicle
- Grain discoloration and yield loss

### Notes

Sheath rot is mainly caused by _Sarocladium oryzae_. It is favored by high humidity, dense planting, and excess nitrogen. The pathogen survives in seeds and crop residues and spreads through wind and rain splash.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Use clean seeds and resistant varieties
- Avoid excessive nitrogen application
- Rotate crops
- Remove infected residues

#### Biological Control

- Promote beneficial microbes that compete with pathogens

#### Chemical Control

- Apply fungicides at booting stage when disease pressure is high

## Sheath Blight

- **Category:** Fungus
- **Stages Mentioned:** Panicle Initiation, Flowering, Milking, Dough

### Symptoms

- Oval or irregular lesions on leaf sheaths near the waterline
- Grayish-green lesions with brown margins that enlarge and merge
- Infected tillers weaken and lodge
- Severe cases can lead to significant yield loss

### Notes

Sheath blight is caused by _Rhizoctonia solani_. It spreads rapidly in fields with high humidity, dense planting, and high nitrogen input. It survives in soil and plant debris and infects rice from tillering to ripening.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Avoid dense planting
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilizer
- Improve drainage and water management
- Remove infected plant debris after harvest

#### Biological Control

- Use antagonistic fungi such as _Trichoderma_

#### Chemical Control

- Apply fungicides when initial symptoms appear, especially under favorable weather

## Birds

- **Category:** Pest
- **Stages Mentioned:** Seedling, Tillering, Stem Elongation, Panicle Initiation, Booting, Flowering, Milking, Dough, Mature
- **Most Destructive Stage Mentioned:** Ripening

### Symptoms

- Missing grains on panicles
- Broken or cut panicles
- Patches of low yield
- Flocks feeding during ripening stage

### Notes

Birds such as maya and other grain-feeding species mainly damage rice during ripening. Damage can be severe in small fields near roosting or nesting areas and under scattered planting schedules.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Practice synchronous planting with neighbors
- Use bird scarers such as flags, ribbons, and noise makers
- Guard fields during ripening
- Harvest promptly

#### Biological Control

- Encourage natural predators such as raptors and owls

## Rice Bug

- **Category:** Pest
- **Stages Mentioned:** Milking, Dough

### Symptoms

- Empty or unfilled grains
- Discolored grains with black spots
- Distorted and shriveled kernels
- Foul odor when bugs are crushed

### Notes

Rice bugs feed on rice grains during milking to ripening by sucking the contents of developing kernels. Continuous cropping and nearby weeds favor buildup.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Plant rice synchronously
- Control weeds around rice fields
- Handpick bugs early in the morning when they are less active

#### Biological Control

- Conserve egg parasitoids and predators such as spiders and assassin bugs

#### Chemical Control

- Apply insecticides only during milking to early ripening if pest density is high

---

## Rice Black Bug

- **Category:** Pest
- **Stages Mentioned:** Flowering, Milking, Dough, Mature

### Symptoms

- Wilting and yellowing of leaves
- Deadheart in seedlings and tillers
- Black shield-shaped insects at the base of plants
- Circular patches of damaged rice in the field

### Notes

Rice black bugs suck sap from rice stems, causing tiller death and poor growth. They are nocturnal and hide during the day. Populations increase in poorly managed fields with continuous planting and abundant weeds. They can move in swarms, especially during early vegetative stages.

### Prevention and Control

#### Cultural and Physical Controls

- Practice synchronous planting and crop rotation
- Plow and flood fields after harvest to expose and kill hiding bugs
- Use light traps to monitor and reduce populations

#### Biological Control

- Conserve natural enemies such as fungi and egg parasitoids

#### Chemical Control

- Apply recommended insecticides only when infestations are severe
